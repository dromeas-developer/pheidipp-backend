# Plan Structure & Visibility
*What the athlete can see of their training plan, and when*

## Macro Plan View

The athlete can always see the full architecture of their training plan — not just today, but the entire journey to their goal. Each phase is shown with its label (base building, threshold development, race specific, taper, race week), its duration in weeks, and its primary training focus.

This macro view serves a specific psychological purpose: it makes individual hard weeks tolerable. An athlete who can see that the current hard block is followed by a recovery week, and that the next phase shifts emphasis, can endure the present effort in context. Without this view, every hard week feels like an arbitrary imposition. With it, the athlete understands the structure they are moving through.

## Future Workout Specificity

Future workouts in the plan show only high-level intent — type of session, approximate duration, training purpose. No specific intervals, no specific targets.

The specific workout is generated on the day from the freshest available data about the athlete's current physiological state. Generating precise interval targets six weeks in advance is false precision: the athlete's threshold will shift, their fatigue state will differ from what was predicted, conditions will vary. Pheidipp does not pretend otherwise.

The near-term preview — the next four to five planned sessions — shows enough for the athlete to mentally prepare and plan their week around the training structure, without committing to targets that will be wrong.

## Phase Transitions

When the plan moves from one phase to the next, the coach acknowledges it explicitly in the daily journal. A brief message explaining what phase is beginning, why the training focus is shifting, and what the athlete should expect in the coming weeks.

These transitions are coaching moments, not silent plan updates. An athlete moving from base building into threshold development should understand why that shift is happening now and what it will feel like. The coach explains it in plain language as part of the normal daily conversation.

## Current Position Visibility

The home view always shows clearly which week and phase the athlete is currently in, how far through the phase they are, and how many weeks remain to the goal event. Simple, always visible, never buried. The athlete never has to navigate to find out where they are in the plan.
